> Here I share my personal opinions about my experience in the First Cloud AI Journey program, to help the FCAJ team improve based on the following aspects:

### Overall Evaluation

**1. Working Environment**
The program gave me a clear way of working: a set of requirements for the report, a workshop website template, and rules for blogs, events and worklogs. The in-person events I attended (FCAJ Community Day at Bitexco Financial Tower, and AWS for Software & Financial Services Companies Day at the Sheraton Saigon Grand Opera) were well organised, and the community around the AWS Study Group made it easy to share what I was learning. After the first two months of team activities, I carried out the 12-week project on my own, so the daily work was mostly independent. It would be nice to have a few more regular gatherings in that phase to keep the team spirit alive.

**2. Support from Mentor / Team Admin**
I received guidance mostly through the program's framework: the report structure, the workshop requirements and the examples to compare against. This pushed me to look for answers in the AWS documentation and in real logs and metrics before asking, and I valued having room to try things and solve problems myself. Because I was working alone in the second phase, short regular check-ins on the major design decisions would have been very helpful.

**3. Relevance of Work to Academic Major**
The project fits my Information Systems major well. I applied what I learned about databases, system design and software engineering to a real application, and learned a lot that is new: serverless architecture, networking and security on AWS, CI/CD, monitoring, and cost control.

**4. Learning & Skill Development Opportunities**
I learned many new skills: designing and deploying a multi-service system on AWS, using least-privilege IAM and cross-account access, monitoring with CloudWatch, building CI/CD with GitHub Actions and CloudFormation, controlling cost, and writing bilingual technical documentation. The events also showed me how AWS approaches AI and agents, including a hands-on workshop where I built a game with Kiro by writing requirements and design first.

**5. Company Culture & Team Spirit**
During the first two months, working with my teammates taught me how to split work, explain a design and give each other feedback. When the program moved to daily worklogs and my teammates had finished, I became the only one left on the project. Even so, the community and events kept me connected, and the experience taught me to take full responsibility for a product from design to operation.

**6. Internship Policies / Benefits**
The AWS credits for the DEV environment let me build and test a real system without worrying about the budget, and the credits provided at the Kiro workshop let everyone build freely for a day. At the same time, I learned that many resources bill by the hour even without users, which made cost control a real part of the work.

---

### Additional Questions

**What did you find most satisfying during your internship?**
Seeing a complete system running on AWS that I built end to end: users can register, log in, create a training plan with an AI coach, chat with a trainer and pay through a sandbox gateway, all behind CloudFront over HTTPS. Setting up the CI/CD pipeline and watching a deployment go through reviewer gates was also very satisfying.

**What do you think the program should improve for future interns?**
Give early guidance on service quotas for new accounts (the Bedrock model quota on my main account was 0, which cost me time), on cost control for hourly-billed resources, and keep some form of check-in for people who end up working alone.

**If recommending to a friend, would you suggest they join this program? Why or why not?**
Yes. It is a place to learn by doing real work on AWS, with a community and events that widen your view. It suits people who like to explore and take ownership of what they build.

---

### Suggestions & Expectations

**Do you have any suggestions to improve the internship experience?**
Provide one complete sample project covering architecture, cost and testing so learners know the expected level of completeness, and keep peer review for the long solo phase.

**Would you like to continue this program in the future?**
Yes. I would like to keep taking part in the community and events, and to continue improving Fitness Assistant.

**Any other comments:**
I plan to keep improving the project after the program: putting chat behind HTTPS on a custom domain, adding automated tests, and going deeper into AI on AWS with Amazon Bedrock and AgentCore. Thank you to the FCAJ team and the community for the opportunity to learn.
